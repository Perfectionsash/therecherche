Lighting is what makes a kitchen feel like a room rather than a showroom. Get it wrong and even the most beautiful cabinetry falls flat. Get it right and the space works at 6am, at 2pm, and at 11pm.

We approach kitchen lighting in three layers. Here's how each one works, and where we use them.

## Task Lighting

This is the light you cook by. Under-cabinet LED strips directed at the worktop are non-negotiable — they eliminate shadows cast by upper cabinets and make prep safe and pleasant. We specify linear LED in pelmets above hanging rails and under-shelf strips on shoe shelves, all on dimmers so you can adjust for the time of day.

Pendant lights over the island are also task lighting — they need to be bright enough for homework, recipes, and casual dining, not just decoration.

Best positioned: Above worktops, island, sink, and hob.

## Ambient Lighting

This is the base layer that fills the room. Recessed downlights are common, but we prefer them sparingly — too many create a clinical effect. Instead, we combine a few well-placed downlights with wall washing or cove lighting that bounces light off the ceiling for a softer, more even glow.

Best positioned: Perimeter of the room, above walkways, dining zones.

## Accent Lighting

This is where lighting becomes design detail. LED strips inside glass-front cabinets, under the island overhang, or inside open shelving highlight materials and create depth at night. Pelmet lighting above tall units draws the eye upward and makes the ceiling feel higher.

Best positioned: Inside cabinetry, under islands, above tall units.

## Colour Temperature

We specify 2700K warm white throughout — it's close to incandescent light and flatters both timber and painted finishes. Cooler 3000K–4000K light can make hand-painted cabinetry look grey and clinical. We also dim everything: bright for cooking, low for evening drinks.

## Controls

Every kitchen we wire has separate circuits for task, ambient, and accent layers, each on its own dimmer. A single scene control by the door lets you recall "Cooking," "Entertaining," or "Night" with one press.

If you're planning a kitchen, bring your lighting ideas to the design visit — we'll walk through the layers and show you how they work together.
