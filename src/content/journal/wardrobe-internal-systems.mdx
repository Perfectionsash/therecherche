The doors of a wardrobe are only half the story. What happens inside — the internal layout, the fittings, the small details — is what determines whether a wardrobe actually works for you day after day.

We design wardrobe interiors around how you live, not just what you own. Here are the systems we find ourselves specifying most often.

## Double Hanging with Pull-Down Rail

For tall rooms, double hanging on both sides maximises shirt and trouser storage. We add a pull-down rail on the short wall for dresses and coats — it brings high-level hanging within easy reach without a step ladder.

Best for: Master bedrooms, shared wardrobes, rooms with high ceilings.

## Angled Shoe Shelves

Standard flat shelves waste vertical space above shoes. Angled shelves at 15–20 degrees let you see every pair at a glance and fit 20–30% more footwear in the same footprint. We integrate LED strips underneath each shelf so the collection is visible even at night.

Best for: Walk-in wardrobes, shoe collectors, compact spaces.

## Leather-Lined Jewellery Drawers

Jewellery and watches need soft, dark storage to avoid scratching and tarnishing. We line oak drawers with leather and add velvet trays, cufflink dividers, and ring rolls — everything has its place, and nothing rattles.

Best for: Walk-in dressing rooms, central islands, high-value collections.

## Central Island with Valet Rod

A central island transforms a walk-in wardrobe from storage into a dressing room. We specify honed marble tops, deep drawers for folded knitwear, and a pull-out valet rod for tomorrow's outfit. It's where you plan your day, not just store your clothes.

Best for: Spacious dressing rooms, couples with shared wardrobes.

## Soft-Close Everything

Drawers, doors, pull-outs — every moving part in our wardrobes uses soft-close dampers. It's a small detail, but it changes how the wardrobe feels every time you use it. No slamming, no noise, just quiet precision.

Best for: Every wardrobe.

## The Layout Process

We start by emptying your current wardrobe — literally everything out — and categorising what stays. Hanging, folded, shoes, accessories, seasonal items. Then we allocate space by frequency of use: daily items at eye level, seasonal storage at the top or bottom, and special-occasion pieces in the most protected compartments.

Every wardrobe we build is made to measure, so there are no filler panels or wasted gaps. The internal system is designed around your actual collection, not a catalogue configuration.
