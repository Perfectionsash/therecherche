A great kitchen isn't about the appliances or the cabinetry — it's about the flow. Get the layout right, and everything else falls into place.

## The Work Triangle (And Why It's Not Enough)

The classic work triangle — sink, hob, fridge — is a starting point, not a rule. Modern kitchens have multiple cooks, prep zones, cleaning zones, drink stations. Think **zones**, not triangles.

## The Five Zones

1. **Consumables** — Fridge, freezer, larder. Near the entrance for grocery unloading.
2. **Non-consumables** — Plates, glasses, cutlery. Near the dishwasher and dining.
3. **Cleaning** — Sink, dishwasher, bin, cleaning supplies. Central, with good light.
4. **Preparation** — Worktop, knives, boards, small appliances. Largest continuous surface.
5. **Cooking** — Hob, oven, extractor, oils, spices. Safety clearance, heat-resistant surfaces.

## Circulation Rules

- **Main aisle**: 1200mm minimum (two people passing)
- **Work aisle**: 1000mm minimum (one person working)
- **Appliance doors**: Add 600mm to appliance depth for open doors
- **Island clearance**: 1100mm on all sides if seating, 1000mm if not

## Common Layouts

**Galley** — Two parallel runs. Efficient, but can feel corridor-like. Best with one end open.

**L-Shape** — Two adjacent walls. Natural zone separation. Good for open-plan.

**U-Shape** — Three walls. Maximum storage, can feel enclosed. Needs width.

**Island + Run** — The London favourite. Island for prep/social, run for storage/cooking. Best of both.

## The Details That Matter

- **Sink centred on window** (or with a view)
- **Dishwasher right of sink** (right-handed) or left (left-handed)
- **Bin near sink, not under it** (pull-out, separated recycling)
- **Extractor ducting planned early** — affects ceiling height, joist runs
- **Sockets where you use them** — island, prep zone, charging drawer
