The worktop is the hardest-working surface in your kitchen. It takes heat, knives, spills, sunlight, and daily abuse — and it needs to look good doing it. Here's how to choose.

## Natural Stone

### Marble (Calacatta, Carrara, Statuario)
**Pros:** Unmatched beauty, each slab unique, stays cool for pastry, ages with character
**Cons:** Porous (stains, etches), requires sealing, softer than alternatives
**Best for:** Bakers, low-traffic kitchens, those who accept patina

### Granite
**Pros:** Extremely durable, heat resistant, huge colour range, lower maintenance than marble
**Cons:** Can look busy, visible seams, heavy
**Best for:** High-traffic family kitchens

### Quartzite
**Pros:** Marble look with granite durability, UV resistant
**Cons:** Expensive, limited colours, still needs sealing
**Best for:** Marble lovers who need performance

## Engineered Surfaces

### Quartz (Silestone, Caesarstone, Compac)
**Pros:** Non-porous, zero maintenance, consistent pattern, huge range
**Cons:** Not heat proof (trivets essential), can look uniform, UV sensitive outdoors
**Best for:** Busy households, rental properties, consistent aesthetic

### Dekton / Neolith / Lapitec (Ultracompact)
**Pros:** Indestructible — heat, scratch, stain, UV proof, thin profiles possible
**Cons:** Expensive, limited edge profiles, brittle on impact
**Best for:** Outdoor kitchens, high-performance needs, minimal joints

## Timber

### Oak, Walnut, Iroko (End-grain or Edge-grain)
**Pros:** Warmth, kindness to knives, repairable, develops patina
**Cons:** Needs oiling, not heat/water proof, shows marks
**Best for:** Prep zones, baking areas, as part of mixed-material scheme

## Our Recommendation

For most London kitchens, we suggest a **primary worktop in ultracompact or quartz** for performance, with a **timber or marble inset** for specific tasks (baking, plating). The best of both worlds.
