Designing a wardrobe is one of those projects that seems straightforward — until you start. The difference between a wardrobe that works and one that frustrates daily comes down to planning. Here's our process.

## 1. Audit What You Own

Before a single measurement, empty your existing storage. Categorise everything: hanging (long/short), folded, shoes, accessories, seasonal, occasional. Be ruthless — if you haven't worn it in a year, it doesn't get space in the new design.

## 2. Measure Twice, Design Once

We measure:
- Floor to ceiling (at multiple points — floors and ceilings are rarely level)
- Wall to wall (including skirting, coving, picture rails)
- Window reveals, radiator positions, socket locations
- Door swing arcs and clearance

## 3. Zone by Frequency

**Daily zone** — Eye level to waist height. Hanging for work clothes, drawers for underwear/socks, shoes you wear weekly.

**Weekly zone** — Just above/below daily. Folded knitwear, second pairs of shoes, handbags in dust bags.

**Seasonal zone** — High level, low level. Vacuum-packed off-season clothing, luggage, rarely used items.

## 4. Choose Your System

**Sliding doors** — Best for tight rooms, no swing clearance needed. Full-height mirrors double the light.

**Hinged doors** — Traditional, allow full access to entire width. Can be painted to match walls.

**Walk-in** — If you have the space (minimum 2.1m × 1.8m), nothing beats it. Add a dressing table, seating, full-length mirror.

## 5. Lighting Is Not Optional

Integrated LED in every compartment. Motion sensors. Warm dimmable light for evening, cooler for morning colour matching. It transforms the experience.

## 6. Future-Proof

Adjustable shelving. Removable dividers. Systems that can be reconfigured as needs change — children growing, working from home, downsizing.
